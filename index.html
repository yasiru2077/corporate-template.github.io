How an engagement runs

Four stages, every time — nothing skipped, nothing that surprises you later.

  1. 01

    Discover

    We map the system you have today, not just the one you want — architecture, data, and the constraints nobody put in the brief.

  2. 02

    Design

    Architecture and interface decisions are made together, before code — so trade-offs get discussed when they're still cheap to change.

  3. 03

    Build

    Small releases, shipped weekly, reviewed by senior engineers — so you see working software early, not a demo at the end.

  4. 04

    Operate

    We stay on to monitor, support, and evolve what we built, with documentation your team can actually use without us.

What teams say after we leave

Questions we get asked

Mostly three shapes: a product that needs to go from idea to production, an integration problem connecting systems that were never meant to talk to each other, or an existing platform that needs to become reliable enough to stop waking someone up at night. If it doesn't fit one of those, ask us anyway — we'll tell you honestly if it's not a fit.

A first release usually ships in 8–12 weeks. Some engagements end there; most continue into an ongoing arrangement where we keep building and operating what we shipped. We scope in phases so you're never committing to more than the next few weeks of work.

Alongside, not instead of. We start with a short discovery period to learn your codebase and conventions before writing anything, then embed on specific workstreams — a new feature, a parallel product line, a reliability backlog — without creating coordination overhead on the work your team already owns.

You choose. Some clients take full ownership with our documentation and a short handover period. Most keep us on for monitoring, on-call support, and continued feature work, billed against the same team that already knows the system.

Engineering studios across three time zones, working as one team on your project rather than a follow-the-sun handoff. You get a single point of contact regardless of who's actually writing the code that week.
